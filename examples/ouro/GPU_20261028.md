The current #78 stage-engine implementation completes three full-depth and
three adaptive Ouro-1.4B BF16/FSDP updates, with exact next-iteration recovery
in a fresh two-rank process. Ten publication modes pass against a fresh policy,
including actual two-device NIXL transfers. This uses the explicit
`publication_outputs` consumer profile; it does not combine #78 and #83 or
establish acceptance of their eventual unified API.

The existing contributor-fork PR remains a draft. Automated experiments do not
attest to human line-by-line review or independent human testing.

## Training and fresh-process recovery

Both runs use four addition prompts, two samples per prompt, response limit 32,
microbatch 1, learning rate 1e-6 and three real optimizer/publication iterations. The existing
first-integer proximity reward exercises GRPO/PPO updates; it is not a held-out
quality or convergence benchmark. Full depth uses threshold 1; adaptive uses 0.

Every valid token passes the predeclared absolute error ≤ 0.5 and ratio [0.5, 2]

The allocated peak is the trainer process's cumulative allocator observation;

## Publication, transport and cache evidence

The ten cases are full, early_exit, prefix, async, graphs, graphs_async,
speculative, graphs_speculative, pd_eager and pd_graphs. Each exercises warm
generation, a full policy publication, a real duplicate-name rejection,
generation fencing, full retry and comparison with a fresh published policy.
Tokens/depths match and selected-score maximum error is 0 in every case.
Per-case single-observation publication times are recorded; they are not a
controlled update-overhead benchmark or training speedup result.

Each P/D producer reports 12,599,296 bytes and 4 actual NIXL writes over warmup
and post-publication requests. Both workers stop with zero KV blocks and the
coordinator has zero reservations. These are same-node correctness results;
no cross-node RDMA or transport-throughput qualification is claimed.

Two independent public profiling captures after publication observe 28 actual
`cudaGraphLaunch` events each, with committed version 1: one ops-only capture
and one full capture with shapes, memory and stacks enabled. The ten-case
matrix's `graphs_enabled` flag alone is not treated as replay evidence.

A separate in-process StageEngine probe uses 32-token prompts at block 16 and
the KV owner's public `lookup_prefix` operation. One cached page is present
before publication and zero immediately after start; generation under the new
weights matches a fresh policy exactly. `num_used_blocks` intentionally excludes
evictable prefix-only blocks, so it is not a cache-entry counter. The initial
counter-based probe failed and its source/log are retained; no engine behavior
was changed to satisfy it. The earlier four-token matrix case enables caching
but cannot itself establish a full cached page.

## Probability-difference attribution

The fixed eight-token controls separate FP32/BF16, Torch/Triton, default/math
SDPA and serial/batched prefill. FP32 selected-score differences are small
(up to about 3.1e-5 in these controls); BF16 differences persist and can change
an argmax on the tested prefix.

In the first physical layer, Q/K/V projections are bitwise identical. Default
SDPA first differs at the attention output entering `o_proj`: maximum 0.00390625,
amplified to 0.125 after projection. Forcing math SDPA makes those first-layer
inputs and outputs exact in this control. Reprojecting each arm's identical
stored input has error 0, isolating the observed initial difference to attention
computation rather than the output projection. This does not prove that every
later-layer or FSDP discrepancy has the same sole cause. Math SDPA does not
establish whole-model BF16 parity; the complete numerical table is retained.

## Matched #130 attention experiment

Protocol: native BF16/Triton, greedy fixed four loops, ignore-EOS, 128 output
tokens, prompts 512/2048 and concurrency 1/4. Both variants use 4096 physical KV
blocks and `min_coda_batch_size=concurrency`. Every cohort reaches a common
first-token barrier before timing; counted decode tokens are exactly C×127.
This isolates fixed-cohort decode, not normal serving arrival latency.

Eight unprofiled runs alternate A/B/B/A/A/B/B/A, four observations per arm.
Loading, warmup, prefill, first-token production and teardown are excluded from
decode timing. Every temporary engine is closed and collected outside timing.

| Prompt / concurrency | Base median tok/s | Candidate median tok/s | Paired median speedup | Observed paired range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 512 / 1 | 17.13 | 18.27 | 1.066× | 1.001–1.102× |
| 512 / 4 | 67.73 | 71.95 | 1.070× | 0.999–1.096× |
| 2048 / 1 | 17.23 | 18.41 | 1.082× | 1.015–1.087× |
| 2048 / 4 | 67.39 | 70.62 | 1.042× | 0.974–1.080× |

All 40 candidate request sequences match the baseline token-for-token; baseline
repeats also agree. The observed medians favor the candidate, but some individual
pairs are neutral/slower. Four paired observations on a shared host do not
establish a universal or statistically guaranteed speedup. GSM8K/task quality,
high-concurrency saturation, other architectures and new-head PD performance
were not measured by this benchmark.

Separate representative 512:C1 profiles include prefill and eight output tokens.
Both ops/full captures observe 768 attention kernels plus 768 separate merge
kernels in the base; the candidate has 768 fused attention kernels and no
separate merge kernel. Total CUDA kernels are 44,450 versus 43,683. This matches
the implementation's launch-removal mechanism; it is not a complete quantitative
decomposition of the wall-time difference. Profiled times are excluded above.

The original 1024-block capacity pilot failed the intended C4 admission contract:
only 127 rather than 508 decode tokens entered its timing window. All pilot
timings were excluded. Its logs and the interrupted preflight's OOM evidence
remain in the archive. The corrected capacity/barrier preflight passed before
the final eight runs began.

## Offline N+2 pilot

The pilot collects 512 depth 1 hidden states from 32 arithmetic prompts with 16
forced-length greedy decode inputs each. Labels predict depth 3 cumulative
hazard under the full-depth teacher's KV history. A linear head is trained for
200 steps; 24 prompt groups train and 8 groups are held out. Feature scaling
uses training groups only. This is N=1→N+2=3 on the frozen four-loop model.

| Hazard threshold | Held-out positives / 128 | Head accuracy | Majority baseline | False positives / negatives |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.2 | 128 | 122/ 128 | 128/ 128 | 0/0 (undefined FPR) |
| 0.5 | 119 | 125/ 128 | 119/ 128 | 1/9 |
| 0.7 | 37 | 128/ 128 | 91/ 128 | 0/91 |

At 0.2 the labels saturate and the trained head loses to a constant prediction;
that regime does not justify adding a predictor. The 0.7 result is a small,
correlated template holdout, not task-quality or deployment evidence. Heads,
preprocessing statistics and trace tensors are retained. Actual early-exit KV
histories can differ from the teacher's; runtime loading/decisions, quality,
latency and N+2 speedup remain unimplemented/unmeasured. This is not Phase3
acceptance.

## Reproduction and remaining boundaries

Use the matching torch/CUDA image and the existing
`requirements-worker.lock`; keep torch inherited. Point PYTHONPATH at one frozen
engine and this fork. The current publication recipe selects the profile explicitly:

```bash
export PYTHONPATH=/path/to/pr78:/path/to/verl
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0,1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=4 python -m torch.distributed.run \
  --standalone --nproc_per_node=2 -m examples.ouro.train \
  --engine-contract publication_outputs --model /path/to/Ouro-1.4B \
  --steps 3 --response-length 32 --prompts-per-step 4 --samples-per-prompt 2 \
  --micro-batch 1 --exit-threshold 1 --output full.json

python -m examples.ouro.validate_publication --model /path/to/Ouro-1.4B \
  --mode pd_graphs --output pd.json
```

training/attention/final-probe job manifests. The command above uses the same
values.
